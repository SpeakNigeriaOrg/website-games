How the path works

Each unit is a scene from a child's day: my day, the body, mealtime, washing, family, home, play, animals, the kitchen. The idea of scenes, and their Yorùbá titles, comes from our curriculum draft; the games are not a copy of that course. A row of stones is one short sitting: a stone that introduces up to four words (the answer can be found on screen, or there are only two choices), a practice stone that mixes in words from earlier sittings, and often a stone for hearing the word's syllables. A unit ends with a "true or silly" check. Earlier words come back on a spacing schedule.

All audio is from our recordings (Femi; Tolani for words Femi has not recorded or where his take did not pass). The path uses lenient quality rules: a recording stays out only for a clear fault (a tone heard high for low, a cut-off start, clipping, a confirmed repeat, poor sound, an unresolved spelling change, or no release rights). Words the curriculum draft lists are marked ●; ○ marks our other words placed in the scene.
