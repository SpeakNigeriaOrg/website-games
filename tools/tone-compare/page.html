Tone timbre comparison

Every clip plays the same measured pitches and the same measured contours — only the timbre differs, and all clips are level-matched so loudness cannot decide it. original is the first six-sine version and soft is what is live now; both are static spectra with a level ramp. Everything below them is an event that evolves — a real attack, and high partials dying faster than low ones — which is the actual difference between a synthesiser and an instrument. Best judged on a phone speaker.

Why the earlier attempts failed. Every one of them shaped a steady state — how the harmonics are balanced — and then put a level ramp on it. That is what an organ or a test tone is. An instrument is an event: a fast, slightly noisy onset, then a spectrum that changes as high partials die away faster than low ones. No amount of re-balancing a static spectrum produces that.

What measuring the speakers' own recordings showed. Their real voices are dark too. Averaged over every syllable clip they recorded, speaker3's energy from 1–2 kHz sits 37 dB below his peak and speaker2's 25 dB below hers; almost everything is in the bottom two octaves. So the original tone was not far off their actual spectral balance, and chasing brightness was chasing the wrong thing. What a real voice has is jitter, shimmer and a soft onset — not treble. The hum voice is built from that: each speaker's own measured resonances (speaker2 at 345/560/818/2799 Hz, speaker3 at 297/1047/1234 Hz), plus a random walk on both pitch and level.

Do not read the numbers across families. Brightness is measured 60 ms after onset, which is fair to both kinds of sound. Crest factor is not shown per row on purpose: a struck sound is supposed to peak hard and decay, so the 9–14 the piano and pluck measure is normal rather than harsh, and the same number that flagged real harshness in the sawtooth means nothing here.

A real tension to listen for. Struck sounds put nearly all their energy in the first 50 ms, which makes the pitch contour much harder to hear — and the contour is what the game teaches. Sustained voices (hum, flute, bowed) keep it fully audible. Piano and marimba may sound nicer in isolation and teach less.

Also worth noting: the hint melody plays tones 290 ms apart, so anything with a long tail (bell at 1.2 s, piano at 0.95 s) will overlap into a chord rather than a sequence. Judge the Dò Re Mí column for that, not the single tones.
